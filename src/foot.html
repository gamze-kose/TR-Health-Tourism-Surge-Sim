About this simulation

A crisis creates care needs that the affected health system cannot meet. In this simulator the user chooses a country and a crisis that could happen there. The model then works out which care needs would arise and how much of them Türkiye's health tourism infrastructure could meet: authorised hospitals, international patient services, transport links, hotels and thermal facilities.

Every scenario is hypothetical. Nothing runs until the user builds a scenario. No scenario describes or predicts a real event, and choosing a country says nothing about that country. A few countries are not offered, so that no scenario can repeat a recent war.

How a scenario is built

Every country is described by the same parameters: population, hospital beds per 1,000 people, human development index, share of people aged 65 or over, diabetes prevalence, road border gates with Türkiye and travel time. The size of a crisis comes from published figures for past events, used only as numbers: people injured in large earthquakes, injuries a day and patients cut off from care in armed conflict, and critical cases in epidemics. A country with fewer hospital beds sends a larger share of severe cases abroad, and the size of a scenario is limited by the population of the country.

How the model works

The model links the chosen country to the Turkish cities that can receive international patients. Each day, referred patients wait for clearance, transport and a coordinator, then move to a province with free capacity of the right kind. Six care lines run underneath: intensive care, surgery and trauma, dialysis, cancer care, rehabilitation and step-down recovery. Patients can block a bed while they wait for the next line.

The reserve has two parts: idle beds in internationally authorised facilities, counted up to a safe occupancy of 85%, and a released share of beds used by paying patients. Thermal and hotel beds provide step-down recovery. Patients go to a city in proportion to its free care places and how close it is.

The results compare options under stated assumptions. They are not forecasts and do not support decisions about individual patients.

Which cities can receive patients

A health facility needs the Ministry of Health's authorisation certificate before it can treat international patients. In December 2024, 650 hospitals held it, in 60 of the 81 provinces. That count is published by region. In each region, provinces named in published sources come first, and the remaining places go to the provinces with the most specialist physicians. The other 21 provinces receive nobody until a unit opens there. Within the 60 provinces patients do not spread evenly: A small group of cities takes most of them, because that is where free places and short routes are.

From 2027 to 2047

The simulation can be set to any year from 2027 to 2047. The crisis stays as the user defines it, while Türkiye changes. Beds, intensive care beds and specialists follow each province's trend for 2015 to 2024, damped year by year so that no past trend runs on for ever. Population and ageing follow the main scenario of TÜİK, and domestic demand for beds rises with them. Three development paths show what can be done: the current course, a planned expansion and an ambitious programme. Their targets are the authors' judgement, except radiotherapy, which uses published estimates of need.

Changing a city yourself

Before a run, any city can be chosen and its capacity changed by hand, or a new unit can be opened there. If nothing is changed, the default values are used: the latest data in the model, carried forward to the chosen year. After a run, the results with the default values and with the changes are shown side by side.

How needs enter the model

Referrals on each care line are split into needs using published injury and referral patterns from past conflicts and disasters. Part of the mix comes from data, and the rest is assumed. The Needs tab shows which shares are which. Heart disease, congenital disease, blood disease and eye disease can be added with one switch.

Not every need can travel. The first hours of emergency care, long-term medicines, mental health care and childbirth stay close to home. Health tourism infrastructure fits the period after that: reconstruction, rehabilitation, prostheses, cancer care, dialysis and recovery.

The people travelling are patients, not tourists. Only the routine care that grows in a long crisis is health tourism in the usual sense. The potential is therefore shown in three parts: how many patients could be treated, how many would actually choose Türkiye, and who would pay.

What the data can and cannot show

  • Country parameters are national averages from the United Nations, the World Bank, the OECD, UNDP and the International Diabetes Federation, compiled by Our World in Data. Hospital beds are estimated for 20 countries.
  • Beds, intensive care, occupancy and workforce in Türkiye are province data from the Ministry of Health yearbooks, 2015 to 2024, read through the open archive of Kaya (CC BY 4.0). Service and workforce columns for 2017 could not be read reliably and are left out.
  • Health tourist shares, authorised beds and rehabilitation beds by province are estimates built from national totals.
  • No province list of burn units, rehabilitation hospitals, prosthetic workshops or paediatric centres was found. Heart surgery centres date from about 2009. The Ministry's list of authorised facilities could not be read by automated tools. Of the 60 receiving provinces, 44 are named in published sources and 16 are inferred from the regional count. Each city panel says which.
  • The evidence is uneven. Figures for some needs, such as face and eye injuries, are thin. No source gives transfer windows in days by need, so most windows are the authors' judgement and are marked as such.
  • No national count of health tourists by nationality or by treatment was found. The routine care stream and the payer shares are assumptions.
  • The record of past disasters and epidemics in city panels mixes peer-reviewed papers, official statements and press reports. It is shown for context and does not enter the calculations.
